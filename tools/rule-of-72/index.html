Rule of 72 Calculator

Estimate how long money takes to double, and compare with the exact answer.

The Rule of 72 is a quick mental shortcut: divide 72 by a yearly growth rate and you get roughly how many years it takes for money to double. Enter a rate and the calculator shows the Rule of 72 estimate next to the exact doubling time, so you can see how close the shortcut is.

How it works

The estimate is simply 72 divided by the rate. The exact figure uses logarithms to find when growth multiplies the starting amount by 2. The shortcut works best for moderate rates. It drifts a little at very low or very high rates, but it is close enough for quick comparisons in your head. The same rule works for anything that grows at a steady rate, including prices under inflation and debt that is not being paid down.

Worked example

At 8.00%, 72 divided by 8 gives 9.0 years, and the exact answer is also 9.0 years. At 12.00%, the estimate is 6.0 years and the exact answer is 6.1 years. The shortcut is off by only a fraction of a year.

  • Rule of 72 Calculator with rate = 12.00%: Rule of 72 estimate (years) = 6.0 years; Exact doubling time (years) = 6.1 years.

Notes

  • Use it in reverse: to double in 6 years you need roughly 12.00% a year.
  • Apply it to debt too; a balance growing at a high rate doubles quickly if left alone.
  • Apply it to inflation to see how fast prices could double.
  • For exact planning, use the exact figure rather than the estimate.

FAQ

Why 72?

It divides evenly by many common rates and happens to give a close answer for typical rates.

How accurate is it?

Very close for moderate rates. The calculator shows the exact answer beside it so you can check.

Does it work for debt?

Yes. Any balance growing at a steady rate doubles in roughly 72 divided by the rate years.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a mental-maths shortcut explained with examples.
