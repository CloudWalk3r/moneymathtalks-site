Inflation Calculator

What something will cost in the future, and what today's money will be worth.

Inflation quietly changes what a number means. The same banknote buys less every year that prices rise. Enter an amount, a yearly inflation rate and a number of years. The calculator shows the future cost of the same things, how much today's money will buy then, and the total price increase.

How it works

Prices are grown by the inflation rate once a year, and each year's rise builds on the last, just like compound interest. That gives the future cost. Running the same maths in reverse gives the buying power: how much of today's shopping the original amount will still cover. The price increase is the future cost minus today's amount. Even moderate inflation adds up over long periods, which is why long-term plans should think in today's money.

Worked example

Take 100.00 at 3.00% inflation for 10 years. The same basket would cost 134.39, a price increase of 34.39, and 100.00 then buys what 74.41 buys today. At 6.00% for 20 years the effect is far bigger. Something costing 1,000.00 today would cost 3,207.14, an increase of 2,207.14, and 1,000.00 would only buy 311.80 of today's shopping.

Notes

  • Compare a low and a higher inflation rate; the gap grows sharply with time.
  • Use this to turn a future goal back into today's money.
  • Savings that earn less than inflation lose buying power even as the balance grows.
  • Official inflation is an average; your own costs may rise faster or slower.

FAQ

What inflation rate should I use?

Recent official figures for your country are a reasonable starting point. Testing a higher rate as well shows the risk.

What does 'today's money will buy' mean?

It shows how much the original amount is worth in today's prices once the years have passed.

Is this the same as compound interest?

The maths is the same; it is just applied to prices instead of savings.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is an educational tool for understanding rising prices.
