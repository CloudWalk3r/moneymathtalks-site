Percentage Change Calculator

The percentage increase or decrease between two values.

Percent changes appear everywhere: prices, pay rises, results and discounts. They are also easy to get wrong in your head. Enter an old value and a new value. The calculator shows the percentage change and the plain difference.

How it works

Percentage change is the new value minus the old value, divided by the old value, times 100. A positive result is an increase and a negative result is a decrease. Because the change is always measured against the old value, a rise and a fall of the same percent do not cancel out. Halving a number needs a fall of half, but getting back needs a rise of 100.00%.

Worked example

Going from 80 to 100 is a difference of 20. Divide 20 by the old value of 80 and multiply by 100, and you get a 25.00% increase.

Notes

  • Always check which number is the starting point; that is what the percent is measured against.
  • A fall followed by a rise of the same percent leaves you below where you started.
  • When rates change, say 'percentage points' for the plain difference between two percents.
  • Use the difference figure when the actual amount matters more than the percent.

FAQ

Why do a rise and a fall of the same percent not cancel?

The second change is measured from a different starting value, so it is a different amount.

What is a percentage point?

The plain gap between two percentages. A rate moving from 4 to 5 percent rises by 1 percentage point, which is a bigger percentage change.

Can the result be negative?

Yes. A negative result means the value went down.
