FIRE Number Calculator

The portfolio size implied by your yearly spending and a chosen withdrawal rate.

FIRE stands for financial independence, retire early. Your FIRE number is the portfolio size that could cover your yearly expenses through withdrawals alone. Enter your yearly expenses and a withdrawal rate. The calculator shows the target portfolio and the monthly spending it supports.

How it works

The target is yearly expenses divided by the withdrawal rate. A lower rate is more cautious and gives a bigger target; a higher rate gives a smaller target but carries more risk of running short. Monthly spending is yearly expenses divided by 12. The withdrawal rate is a rule of thumb based on historical returns, not a guarantee, so many people test more than one rate.

Worked example

With yearly expenses of 30,000.00 and a 4.00% withdrawal rate, the target portfolio is 750,000.00. That supports spending of 2,500.00 a month.

  • FIRE Number Calculator with expenses = 30,000.00, withdrawal = 4.00%: Target portfolio = 750,000.00; Monthly spending = 2,500.00.

Notes

  • Lowering yearly expenses shrinks the target as well as making it easier to save.
  • Test a more cautious withdrawal rate to see how the target changes.
  • Include costs that may rise later, such as health insurance or home repairs.
  • The number is a planning target, not a guarantee of safety.

FAQ

What is a withdrawal rate?

The share of the portfolio you take out each year to live on.

Is 4 percent safe?

It is a widely discussed rule of thumb, not a promise. Markets and timing can change the outcome.

Should I include inflation?

Enter expenses in today's money; the withdrawal rate idea already assumes spending rises with prices.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is an educational planning tool.
