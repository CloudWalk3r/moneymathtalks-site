2% Cashback vs 24% APR: When a Rewards Card Costs You

Intro

Your card pays you 2% back, but what does one carried balance at 24% cost you? Cashback feels like a reward for spending, and when you pay the statement in full, it really is. But the moment a balance rolls over, the math flips. We'll price the rewards first, then carry one month, then three, and watch what happens to the cashback. The last number is the one that changes how most people use the card. These examples assume 2% cashback on $1,000 a month, a 24% APR, fixed payments, and no new spending on a carried balance. First, what the rewards really pay.

What 2% back pays

So how much does 2% back actually put in your pocket? It sounds small, and it is, so let's see what it adds up to over a year. Cashback is a small percent of what you spend, handed back to you. It isn't extra income. It's a discount on purchases you were going to make anyway. That's why it only helps when two things are true: the spending is spending you'd do regardless, and the statement gets paid in full. Buy more just to chase rewards, and the discount quietly turns into extra cost. Here's the math. Say you spend $1,000 a month on the card. At 2% back, that's $20 a month. Do that for 12 months, and $20 times 12 is $240 a year. That's real money. It might cover a utility bill, or a nice dinner out a few times a year. And it costs you nothing extra, as long as every statement is paid off by the due date. Keep that $240 in mind, because it's the number everything else gets measured against. So a rewards card paid in full is a small, steady win, about $240 a year. It won't change your life, but it's worth having. But what happens to that win if one month's statement doesn't get paid in full?

Carry one month

Here's what's at stake: one month of not paying in full. Does a single carried balance just dent your cashback, or eat most of the year? Cashback and interest look alike, since both are percents, but they work very differently. Cashback is 2%, paid once, on what you spend. Interest at 24% a year is charged on what you owe, which works out to about 2% every single month, again and again until the balance is gone. So a balance doesn't just cancel one month's reward. It charges you that same 2%, month after month. Let's run it. Say one month's $1,000 doesn't get paid off, and it sits on the card at 24%. You pay $100 a month toward it, with no new spending added. At that pace, it takes 12 months to clear. Over those 12 months, the interest comes to about $127. Now compare. A whole year of cashback added up to $240. One carried month costs about $127, more than half of that whole year, gone to interest. So one slip doesn't ruin you, but it eats more than half a year of rewards. For that year, the card stops paying you and starts charging you. And slips like that rarely stay at one month. What if the balance keeps growing?

Carry three months

So what happens if you carry three months of spending instead of one? Does the cost just triple, or does it grow faster than that? With a bigger balance, the same payment does less. Each month, interest takes its cut first, and only what's left actually lowers what you owe. On a small balance, most of your payment goes to the debt itself. On a larger one, more of that same payment gets swallowed by interest, so the balance shrinks slowly and the months stack up. Here are the numbers. Let the balance build to $3,000, three months of spending, still at 24%. You keep paying $100 a month, and you stop adding to it. Now it takes 47 months to clear. That's almost four years of payments. And the interest comes to about $1,627. Three times the balance, but nearly four times as long, and more than 12 times the interest of carrying just one month. So the cost doesn't grow in a straight line. Let a balance build, and the interest grows much faster than the balance itself. Which brings us back to the question from the start: what does carrying a balance really do to your cashback?

The biggest saving

At the start I asked what a carried balance at 24% costs you against 2% cashback. Here's the answer I promised. Paid in full, $1,000 a month on the card earns $20 a month, or $240 a year. Carry $3,000 at 24%, paying $100 a month, and it takes 47 months to clear, with about $1,627 in interest. That's close to seven years of cashback, gone. So the rule is simple. Rewards only pay if the balance is paid in full every month. Setting up an automatic payment for the full statement makes that easy. And if you're carrying a balance right now, the 2% doesn't matter much. Clearing the balance does, and it's worth more than any reward the card can offer.

Recap

Quick recap. Paid in full, 2% back on $1,000 a month is $240 a year. Carry one month's balance, and it takes 12 months and about $127 of interest to clear.
