What Your Daily Coffee Really Costs You

One year of $5 a day

So what does a $5 coffee really cost you? Not today, but over a whole year of mornings. It's easy to work out, and easy to miss. Small daily spending hides well. Each coffee is small, and each one feels separate. You never see the total, because it never shows up as one bill. This isn't about giving up coffee. If it makes your day better, it may be worth every cent. The point is to know its real price. Then you choose it on purpose, not out of habit. Here's the math. $5 a day is about $150 a month, counting about thirty days. Over a year, that's $150 times 12 months. That comes to $1,800, all from one small habit. That's about the price of a short trip or a new laptop. You never got one bill of $1,800 in the mail. Instead, it left your account $5 at a time. That's the real price of Maya's coffee: about $1,800 a year. You'd expect a few dollars a morning to just vanish. Instead, they add up to one big bill a year. But a year goes by fast. What does the same $5 a day add up to over 10 years?

Ten years of it

Here's what's at stake. A small daily cost doesn't stop after one year. It often keeps going for many years. So what do 10 years of $5 a day really add up to? There are two ways to look at a long habit. The first is simple: add up what you spent. The second asks a new question. What if that money had gone somewhere it could grow? That's what investing means. You put money in, and over time it can earn more money. Then that extra earns money too. Over a few years, the gap is small. Over many years, it becomes the biggest part of the story. Let's run both. $150 a month over 10 years is $150 times 120 months. That's $18,000 spent. Now say the same $150 a month was invested instead. Say it grows about 7% a year. After 10 years, it's worth about $25,960. So the same money is either $18,000 that's gone, or about $25,960 that's still yours. The only difference is where each $5 went. You might think 10 years of coffee costs $18,000. It actually costs more. That money could have grown to about $25,960 instead. And 10 years is still only the start. What happens if the habit lasts 30 years?

Thirty years, invested

Lots of daily habits last most of a working life. So what does $5 a day become over 30 years, if it's invested instead of spent? This is the number from the start. Time is what makes the biggest difference. In the early years, most of the money is just what you put in. Later, what it earned starts earning too. Then each year adds more than the year before. That's why a small amount over many years can beat a big amount over a few. The years just keep stacking up. Here's the math. Put in $150 a month, 30 years in a row. If it grows about 7% a year, it reaches about $183,000. Now split that in two. You put in $54,000 of your own money. Growth adds about $129,000. So more than two thirds of it came from growth, not from you. And it all started as $5 a day, the price of one coffee. So most of that money was never coffee money. You'd expect Maya's own $54,000 to be the big part. Instead, growth adds about $129,000. That's more than twice what she put in. So let's go back to that one coffee, and the number I promised at the start.

The biggest saving

At the start, I asked what one small coffee is really worth over a working life. Here's the answer I promised. Count Maya's $5 a day as $150 a month. Say she invests it, and it grows about 7% a year. After 30 years, it reaches about $183,000. She'd put in $54,000, and growth adds about $129,000. You'd expect a coffee habit to cost a few thousand dollars at most. Instead, the same money could grow to more than three times what she spent. This doesn't mean Maya can never buy a coffee again. It means every daily habit has a price. Keep the ones you love. Rethink the ones you don't even notice anymore.

Recap

Quick recap. $5 a day is about $150 a month, or $1,800 a year. Over 10 years, that's $18,000 spent. Invested at about 7%, it could be about $25,960 instead.
