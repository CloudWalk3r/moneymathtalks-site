How $100 Extra a Month Can Cut Years Off Your Mortgage

Intro

You pay $1,331 a month over 30 years, so why do you never see the interest? Meet Olivia. She owes $200,000 on her home. Her rate is 7%, and she has 30 years to go. Inside those payments hides a huge pile of interest. So can a small extra payment really change that? We'll try $100 extra, then $300 extra. The first one alone is worth staying for. These numbers are just an example. The rate stays at 7% the whole time. She pays the extra every month. There are no fees. First, the normal loan.

The 30-year payment

So what does a $200,000 home loan really cost? Everyone talks about the payment. But the interest is the number that matters. First, what is interest? It's what the bank charges you to borrow. Each month, the bank adds interest on what you still owe. Your payment pays that interest first. Only what's left pays down the loan. Early on, you owe the most. So most of each payment is interest. The loan shrinks very slowly at first. That's why a long loan costs so much. Here's the normal loan. Olivia borrows $200,000 at 7%, over 30 years. Her payment is about $1,331 a month. That's 360 payments. Over those 360 months, about $279,000 goes to interest. That's more than she borrowed. So she pays back the whole loan, plus all that interest on top. She pays it a little at a time, over 30 years. You'd expect the $1,331 payment to be the big number. It isn't. The big one is the interest, about $279,000. It hides inside all 360 payments. So what if she adds just $100 to every payment? How much of that interest goes away?

Add $100 a month

Here's what's at stake. Years of payments, and tens of thousands of dollars. And $100 a month might already fit your budget. So what does it really do? Here's the trick. When you pay extra, none of it goes to interest. It all pays down the loan. So you owe a little less. Next month, the interest is a little smaller. So more of your normal payment pays down the loan too. This keeps going, month after month. That's why a small extra does more than you'd think. One tip: ask your bank to put the extra on the loan itself. Let's try it. Olivia pays $100 extra each month. So she pays $1,431 instead of $1,331. Now the loan is paid off in 291 months. Before, it took 360. That's 69 months sooner. That's almost six years with no house payment at all. And she pays about $63,000 less in interest. All from $100 a month. You'd expect $100 to barely matter on a loan this big. Instead, it takes off almost six years. And about $63,000 stays with Olivia, not the bank. But what if she can pay a bit more? What happens with $300 extra every month?

Add $300 a month

If $100 does that much, $300 should do even more. So how far does it go on the same $200,000 loan? It works the same way, just faster. Every extra dollar cuts what you owe. And that cuts the interest that comes after. But paying extra is a choice, not a must. Money you put into your home is hard to get back out. So first, keep some savings for surprises. Pay off any credit cards too, since they usually charge more. After that, extra house payments are a sure way to save. Here are the numbers. Olivia pays $300 extra each month. That's $1,631 in total. Now the loan is done in 217 months. That's almost 12 years sooner. Her interest drops from about $279,000 to about $152,000. So she keeps about $127,000. Same house, same rate, same loan. The only change is $300 more a month. So the extra payment is like a dial Olivia controls. At $300 extra, she keeps about $127,000. That's close to half of all the interest. So let's go back to the start. What does $100 a month really save?
