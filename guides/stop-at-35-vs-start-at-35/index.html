Invest for 10 Years and Stop, or Start at 35 and Never Stop?

Intro

You think waiting until 35 is safe, but what do you lose by skipping 10 years? Meet Ella. She's 25, and she plans to start investing later, at 35, once she earns more. Waiting feels free. But those 10 years could leave a gap she never closes by 65, even if she invests every month after that. So which path wins for Ella: start now and stop at 35, or wait and never stop? The answer flips what most people expect. Both paths invest $500 a month at an assumed 7%, the same every year. Real markets swing, so the gap is an illustration, not a promise. It's also before taxes, fees and inflation. First, path one.

Ten years, then stop

Path one: Ella starts now. What does $500 a month add up to by the time she turns 35? Here's how it works. Every month, Ella's money buys a small piece of the market instead of sitting in her account. Each piece can grow, and then the growth starts growing too. That's compounding. In the first years it's slow. Most of the balance is simply what she put in, with a thin layer of growth on top. Here's the math. Ella invests $500 a month from 25 to 35, at an assumed 7%. After those 10 years, she has about $86,540. Now split that in two. She put in $60,000 of her own money. Growth adds about $26,540 on top. So most of the pot is still her own deposits. Then, at 35, she stops for good. You'd expect ten years of saving to build a fortune. Instead, it's a modest pot, and growth is less than half of what Ella put in. On its own, it doesn't look like a winning plan. But Ella doesn't touch it. What can that pot do alone, with no new deposits, for the rest of her working life?

Left alone until 65

Here's what's at stake. If Ella waits, those first 10 years of growth are gone for good. So what does that early pot become if she just leaves it alone until 65? Here's the idea. When you stop adding money, the pot doesn't stop growing. Each year, the growth is a share of everything already there. So a bigger pot earns more, and that extra earns too. Early on, the gains are small. Later, they're large, because they're working on decades of earlier growth. Time does the heavy lifting, not new deposits. Let's run it. At 35, Ella has about $86,540, and she stops. She never adds another dollar. The pot just stays invested, at the same assumed 7%. By 65, it could reach about $702,000. That's 30 years of growth on a pot she finished building at age 35, and everything after that last deposit came from time alone. You'd expect the money to stall the day Ella stops adding. Instead, it keeps going, and by 65 it could reach about $702,000. Her last deposit was the day she turned 35. But Ella's other path looks safer on paper: wait, earn more, and never stop. Could all those extra deposits catch up?

Start at 35 instead

Path two: Ella waits until 35, then invests the same $500 every month, all the way to 65. With 30 years of deposits instead of 10, surely she comes out ahead? Here's the second path. Ella waits until 35, then starts investing and never stops. On paper, it looks safer. She'd be older, earning more, and saving for much longer. Every month, a fresh deposit goes in. The catch is that her first dollar starts growing 10 years later. And those missing years are the ones that would have compounded the longest. Here's the math. Ella starts at 35 instead, and invests $500 a month until 65, at the same assumed 7%. By 65, it reaches about $610,000. Now look at what she put in. That's $180,000 of deposits, 3 times as much as path one. And it still ends up about $92,000 less. You'd expect three times the deposits to win easily. Instead, Ella puts in $180,000 on this path and still finishes about $92,000 behind the path where she stopped at age 35. So let's go back to Ella at 25, and to the question we started with.

The biggest saving

At the start I asked which path wins for Ella by 65: start now and stop at 35, or wait and never stop. Here's the answer I promised. Starting now wins. Ten years of $500 a month, then nothing, could reach about $702,000. Waiting until 35 and investing every month to 65 reaches about $610,000. That's $180,000 of deposits, 3 times as much, and still about $92,000 less. You'd expect the path with three times the saving to finish ahead. Instead, the ten years Ella thought she could skip end up worth more than all 30 years of saving after them. And she doesn't have to choose. Starting now and never stopping beats both paths.

Recap

Quick recap. Ten years of $500 a month, at an assumed 7%, grows to about $86,540. Left alone until 65, that pot could reach about $702,000. Starting at 35 and never stopping reaches about $610,000. That's three times the deposits, and still about $92,000 less. The early years do the most work. If this changed when you plan to start, subscribe.
