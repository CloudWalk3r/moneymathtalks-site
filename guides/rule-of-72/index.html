How Long Until Your Money Doubles? The Easy Trick

Intro

Your savings take 12 years to double at 6%, so why does debt never wait that long? Meet Ethan. Ethan has some savings that grow slowly. He also owes money on a credit card he's left alone for a while. He checks how much he has and how much he owes. But he never checks the rates. What he stands to lose is years. His savings crawl, while his debt grows all by itself. So how fast does each one really double? One simple rule tells you in seconds. And the last rate we test is the one most people never check. The rule is called the rule of 72. We'll try it at three example rates: 6%, 2% and 24%. The exact answers assume growth is added once a year. First, how the rule works.

How the rule works

So how long does it really take your money to double? You don't need a computer to find out. You need one number and one simple division. Here's the rule. Take 72 and divide it by the rate. The rate is how much your money grows in a year, as a percent. The answer is about how many years it takes to double. It works because your money earns money, and then that earns money too. Each year, the growth is added on. So the next year grows from a bigger amount. The rule of 72 is a shortcut for all of that. You can do it in your head, anywhere. Let's test it. Say your money grows about 6% a year. 72 divided by 6 is 12. So it doubles in about 12 years. The exact answer is 11.9 years, so the shortcut is very close. Every dollar you leave growing at 6% becomes two dollars in about 12 years. It becomes four dollars in about 24 years. Keep going, and it's eight dollars after 36 years. Each doubling is bigger than the one before. So at 6%, Ethan's money doubles in about 12 years. The exact answer is 11.9. You'd expect a shortcut you do in your head to be rough. Instead, it's almost exact. But what if your money earns much less? What does a low rate like 2% really mean?

2% vs 6%

Here's what's at stake. A rate that looks only a little lower can add decades to your wait. So how long does money take to double at just 2%? A low rate can feel close enough. 2% and 6% don't look far apart. But the rule of 72 shows something surprising. The gap in years is far bigger than the gap in the rates. The higher the rate, the faster your money doubles. So a small change in the rate can mean a huge change in how long you wait. That's why it's worth knowing what your savings really earn. Here are the numbers. At 2% a year, 72 divided by 2 is 36. So the same money takes about 36 years to double. That's 24 years longer than at 6%, just to double once. Here's another way to see it. In those 36 years, money at 2% doubles once. At 6%, it doubles three times. That's eight times the money, instead of two. You'd think 2% and 6% are close. They aren't. At 2%, money takes about 36 years to double. That's 24 years longer. In that time, 6% doubles it three times. So far, the rule has worked for you. But it works just as well against you. What happens with debt?

When debt doubles

The rule of 72 works on any money, yours or someone else's. So what happens to credit card debt at 24% if you leave it alone? Debt grows the same way savings do. The card charges interest. That's what you pay for borrowing. The interest gets added to what you owe. Then next month, you're charged interest on the bigger amount. Savings often grow slowly, and card debt often grows fast. So debt can pull ahead of your savings. The rule of 72 makes that easy to see. It turns the rate on your bill into a deadline you can feel. Here's the math. Say a card charges 24% a year, and you pay nothing off. 72 divided by 24 is 3. So the debt doubles in about 3 years. The exact answer is 3.2 years, so the rule is close again. What you owe could double in about 3 years. Then it could double again about 3 years later. Savings at 2% take 36 years to double just once. So the same rule tells Ethan two very different stories. His savings at 2% take 36 years to double. His card debt at 24%, left alone, doubles in about 3 years. Let's go back to the start, and what the rule of 72 says about debt.

The biggest saving

At the start, I asked how fast Ethan's savings and his debt really double. Here's the answer I promised. Say his card charges 24% a year. 72 divided by 24 is 3. Left alone, what he owes doubles in about 3 years. It's the same rule that gives 12 years at 6%, and 36 years at 2%. You'd expect savings to be the number in Ethan's life that grows fastest. It isn't. It's the debt. That's why card debt with a high rate is usually the first thing to pay off. Run the rule on every rate you have. Check what you earn and what you owe. It takes seconds. And it shows where time works for you, and where it works against you.

Recap

Quick recap. Divide 72 by the yearly rate to see how long it takes to double. At 6%, it's about 12 years. At 2%, it's about 36 years. That's 24 years longer. And card debt at 24% doubles in about 3 years. Check every rate you earn, and every rate you pay. If this rule was new to you, subscribe.
