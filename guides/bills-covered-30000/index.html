Every $100 Monthly Bill Needs $30,000 Invested to Cover It

Intro

Your $100 monthly bill needs $30,000 invested to cover it each month, so why do you never cut it? Meet Nadia. Her bills come to $3,000 a month, and she wants her investments to pay them one day. If she only adds to the pile, she could spend 5 years investing to cover a single bill. So how much does Nadia really need, and is investing more the only way there? The last step doesn't invest a single dollar, and it still moves her finish line. First, the assumptions. The 4% rule is a rule of thumb from studies of long retirements, not a promise. Growth is an assumed 7% a year, and real markets swing. Everything is in today's dollars, before taxes and fees, and bills rise with inflation. Four steps. First, one bill.

One $100 bill

What would it take for your investments to pay a $100 monthly bill, every single month? The answer isn't printed on the bill. The 4% rule is a rule of thumb for living off investments. You take out about 4% of your pile in the first year, then keep that amount rising with prices. So work backward. Take what a bill costs in a year, and divide by 4%. Here's the math. A bill of $100 a month, paid 12 times, is $1,200 a year. Now divide that $1,200 by 4%. Under the 4% rule, covering that one bill from investments takes about $30,000. A bill you barely notice needs a pile that size, just to pay it month after month. You'd expect a small bill to need a small cushion. Instead, it needs about $30,000 invested. Every bill you keep has a pile like that attached. But nobody has just one bill. So what happens when you price every bill Nadia pays?

All your bills

Here's what's at stake for Nadia. Her bills are $3,000 a month, and she wants investments to pay them all. How big is that pile? The math doesn't change when the bills get bigger. Rent, power, phone, insurance, groceries: add them into one monthly number. Multiply by 12, then divide by 4%, just like before. Every dollar of bills needs the same amount invested behind it. Let's run it. Nadia's bills of $3,000 a month, times 12, are $36,000 a year. Divide that $36,000 by 4%. Covering all of them takes about $900,000 invested. That's her full target. Remember, it's a rule of thumb, not a promise. A bad stretch in the market can change the picture. You'd expect Nadia's target to depend on what she earns. It doesn't. It depends only on her bills: about $30,000 for every $100 a month. That target is a long way off. So what does building just the first $30,000 take?

Building the first $30,000

How much would Nadia invest each month to build that first $30,000 in 5 years? It's more than the bill itself. Building a pile takes two things: your deposits, and the growth on them. Early on, deposits do nearly all the work, because there isn't much money to grow yet. Over a short stretch, most of the pile is simply money you put in yourself. Here are the numbers. To build the first $30,000 in 5 years, at an assumed 7% a year, you'd invest about $419 a month. You'd put in about $25,140 of your own money. Growth adds about $4,860 on top. And 7% is only an assumed average. Some years run ahead, and others fall behind. You'd expect growth to do the heavy lifting. Instead, about $25,140 comes out of Nadia's own pocket, all to cover a single $100 monthly bill. Five years of investing, for one bill. Is there another way to deal with it?

Cut one bill instead

What if Nadia didn't cover that bill at all? What if she cut a $100 monthly bill for good? The target is built from your bills, not your income. So there are two ways to close the gap. You can raise the pile until it reaches the target. Or you can lower the target. A bill you no longer pay needs no money standing behind it. Watch the numbers move. Cut a $100 monthly bill for good, and Nadia's yearly bills fall from $36,000 to $34,800. Divide by 4% again. Her target drops from $900,000 to $870,000. That's $30,000 she no longer needs: the same amount that took 5 years to build. But it only works if the bill stays gone. You'd expect cutting $100 a month to save just $100 a month. It does more than that. It also takes $30,000 off the target. So let's go back to Nadia, and to the question from the start.

The biggest saving

At the start I asked how much Nadia really needs, and whether investing more is the only way there. Here's the answer. To cover $3,000 of monthly bills under the 4% rule, she needs about $900,000. But investing more is not the only way. Cut a $100 monthly bill for good, and her target drops from $900,000 to $870,000. That's $30,000 she no longer needs. You'd expect one small bill to barely matter next to a target that size. Instead, cutting it does the same job as 5 years of investing $419 a month. The finish line also moves with what you stop spending.

Recap

Quick recap. A monthly bill of $100 takes about $30,000 invested to cover. All of Nadia's bills, at $3,000 a month, take about $900,000. Building the first $30,000 in 5 years takes about $419 a month, at an assumed 7%. And cutting a $100 monthly bill for good lowers the target to $870,000.
