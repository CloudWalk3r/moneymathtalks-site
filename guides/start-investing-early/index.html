Why Starting to Invest 10 Years Earlier Is Worth So Much

Intro

You could turn $200 a month into about $525,000, but what does waiting cost you? That's 40 years of steady investing at an assumed 7%. And waiting a few years to start costs far more than most people guess. Starting late feels harmless, because there always seems to be time to catch up. In the next few minutes we'll work through one example, test what happens when you wait, and see what catching up really takes. Keep the last number in mind. We'll assume 7% average growth a year, compounded monthly, with no fees or taxes. That's an assumption, not a promise. Three tests: 40 years of investing, 30 years, and what it takes to catch up. Let's start with 40.

$200 a month for 40 years

So what does $200 a month really turn into if you leave it alone for 40 years? Most people guess far too low the first time. Here's the idea. When your money grows, the growth starts growing too. Early on it feels slow, because most of the balance is just what you put in. But every year the pile is bigger, so the same rate adds more dollars. Over a long stretch, growth does most of the work, not your deposits. Time is the one ingredient you can't buy back later. Let's run it. $200 a month for 40 years, growing at an assumed 7% a year, reaches about $525,000. Now split that total in two. You put in $96,000 of your own money over those 40 years. Growth adds about $429,000. So more than four out of every five dollars at the end came from growth, not from you. That's the payoff of starting early. You didn't need a big salary or a lucky pick. You needed a steady $200 a month and a lot of time. But what if you wait? What does the same plan look like if you start 10 years later?

Start 10 years later

This is where waiting gets expensive. Same $200 a month, same assumed 7%, but you start 10 years later. How much of that $525,000 is left? Waiting removes your first ten years of deposits, and those are the dollars with the longest time to grow. Each of them would have kept compounding for decades. Skip them, and you lose the growth that does the heavy lifting at the end. The late dollars you still put in never get that much time. That's why a short delay costs so much more than it looks. Here's the maths. Start 10 years later and the same $200 a month reaches about $244,000. Compare that with the $525,000 from before. That's about $281,000 less, from just $24,000 less put in. You kept $24,000 by waiting, and it cost you about $281,000 at the end. Ten years of waiting looks small on a calendar. In this example it cuts your final balance by more than half. The delay is the most expensive part of the plan. So can you just invest more later and catch up? Let's see what that would take.

What catching up costs

Say you start 10 years late but still want the same $525,000. How much would you need to put in every month to get there? Catching up means asking fewer years to do the same job. With less time, growth has less room to work, so your own deposits have to carry more of the load. It's possible, but the monthly number climbs quickly. That's the real price of waiting. It's not that the goal can't be reached, it's that it gets harder every year you put it off. Here's the number. To reach the same $525,000 in 30 years, you'd need about $430 a month. That's more than double the $200 a month you'd need if you started 10 years earlier. Same goal, same assumed growth, but the late start asks you for more than twice as much every single month, for 30 years. So waiting doesn't just shrink the result. If you still want the same result, it makes every month of the plan cost more than twice as much. Now let's go back to the start, and the one number that ten years of waiting really costs.

The biggest saving

At the start I said waiting costs far more than most people guess. Here's the number I promised. Start 10 years later, with the same $200 a month and the same assumed 7%, and you end up with about $281,000 less, from just $24,000 less put in. That's the whole cost of the delay. Not a fee, not a bad investment, just time you didn't use. And the fix isn't complicated. Start with whatever you can, even if it's small, and let the years do their work. The earliest dollars are the ones that grow the most.

Recap

Quick recap. $200 a month for 40 years reaches about $525,000, mostly from growth. Start 10 years later and it reaches about $244,000. And catching up takes about $430 a month.
